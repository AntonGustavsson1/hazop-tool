SECTION 4: SYSTEMIC PATTERNS & ROOT CAUSES

PATTERN A: Data Entry Abandonment (75% of records)
-----------
Evidence:
  - 3 out of 4 records have no consequence steps
  - Initial descriptions range from cryptic codes to equipment tags
  - No progression apparent from incomplete to complete

Possible Causes:
  1. User Interface Issues
     - Consequence step entry dialog may be confusing
     - No clear indication of what to enter in each "Del"
     - No templates or examples provided

  2. User Training Gap
     - Users may not understand HAZOP consequence definition
     - May confuse equipment references with consequences
     - May not understand 5-step structure requirement

  3. System Issue
     - Data migration incomplete (old system → new system)
     - Bulk import with missing step data
     - Database corruption or incomplete sync

  4. Time Pressure
     - HAZOP team may have rushed to create records
     - Intended to return and complete chains (but didn't)
     - Deadlines or scope changes may have interrupted work

Recommended Investigation:
  - Interview HAZOP team members: "When were records 2-4 created and by whom?"
  - Check database creation timestamps
  - Review any bulk import logs
  - Examine version control history (if exists)

PATTERN B: Inconsistent Terminology
-----------
Observations:
  - Record 1: Uses clear Swedish descriptions ("Låg nivå", "Processtörning")
  - Records 2-3: Use abbreviations/codes ("O/CO/C", "11VI:4304")
  - Suggests different people entering data OR different entry times

Implication:
  - Quality control was not consistent
  - No data entry validation was enforced
  - Different versions of "how to fill this in" may have circulated

PATTERN C: Missing Severity Assessment
-----------
Observation:
  - All records have severity = 1 (numeric value)
  - But initial descriptions show wide range of potential impacts
  - No apparent correlation between description and severity

Example:
  - "Ventil stängd / delvis stängd" (valve fail) = severity 1
  - "Reglerventil felar stängd" (control valve fail) = severity 1
  - These could represent very different risk levels depending on process

Question:
  - Were severity levels assigned, or defaulted to "1"?
  - Should they be different for each unique consequence?
